---

## How this should sound

Like a person sharing their screen on a call. Not an ad, not a documentary.

- **Talk about what's on screen as it happens.** "There's the pop-up" beats
  "the pop-up appears".
- **Contractions and fillers are good here.** "Okay so", "now", "look", "and
  there it is". They're what makes it sound like a person.
- **React.** Line 8 and line 11 are the two moments worth reacting to.
- Don't push. The product is doing the arguing; the voice just points at it.

**About the name:** line 1 says "Lisa here". Change it to whoever you like, but
**it has to match the voice you pick** — a male voice saying "Lisa here" ruins
the take in the first two seconds. It's the only place the name appears.

If you use the same voice as before (Alex Wright), say "Alex here" or drop the
name: "Hey — let me show you what Right Lead does for a law firm."

## Length

The previous script ran exactly 1:00. **This one will land around 1:15**, because
talking to someone takes longer than narrating at them. That's the trade.

If you want it back under a minute, tell me and I'll drop two scenes — the clip
or the campaigns table are the ones that can go without breaking the story.
Don't fix it by reading fast.

## The script

| # | What's on screen | Line |
|---|------------------|------|
| 1 | Title card | Hey — Lisa here. Let me show you what Right Lead actually does for a law firm. |
| 2 | A blog post on the firm's site | So this is the firm's blog. Someone's reading it — they came in from an ad. |
| 3 | The pop-up notice appears | And there's the pop-up. Notice it's not the form. It just asks. |
| 4 | She taps it, the form opens | She taps it, and now it asks what her case is about. |
| 5 | Picks employment → workplace injury | Employment, workplace injury. From here it only asks what that case needs. |
| 6 | Fills it in and sends | Name, email, what happened to her. And she sends it. |
| 7 | The request in the panel | And here it is on the firm's side. Every answer, and how urgent she says it is. |
| 8 | The abandoned tab | Now — most people never get that far. That's the part everybody loses. |
| 9 | Her row, with the step she reached | Not here. You've got her email and the exact step she stopped at. |
| 10 | Reminder sent, the email | One reminder goes out, with a link straight back to her own form. |
| 11 | The form reopens, already filled | And look — everything she typed is still there. |
| 12 | The pop-ups screen | The form can live in a pop-up. Each one has its own snippet and its own numbers. |
| 13 | The clip on the site | Or in a little video of you, down in the corner. |
| 14 | The request's contact card | And every request tells you where it came from. Right down to the blog post. |
| 15 | The campaigns table | So you can see which campaign is actually bringing you cases. |
| 16 | Closing card | That's Right Lead. Have a look. |

Line 8 is the turn in the whole thing — "most people never get that far".
Take a beat before it.
